## 1. Video 12: Weekly Halving Theory II

### A. Determining the Weekly Split
The "Split" determines how the five trading days of the week are divided to identify the high or low of the week. This division is strictly derived from the **Economic Calendar** for that specific week.

*   **Core Principle:** News events induce volatility. Where volatility is more compact on one side of the week, less time is required for the High or Low of the Week to occur.
*   **The 3/2 Split (Most Common):**
    *   **Structure:** First Half (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday) / Second Half (Thursday, Friday).
    *   **News Setup Example:** News events occur on Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday.
    *   **Logic:** At the beginning of the week (Monday–Wednesday), there is only 1 news event (Tuesday). At the end of the week (Thursday–Friday), there are 2 news events (Thursday, Friday). Since volatility is concentrated at the end of the week, it requires *more time* (3 days) at the beginning of the week to establish the high or low.
    *   **Default Rule:** If news events are evenly spread out or you are in doubt, **always assume it is a 3/2 split**.
*   **The 2/3 Split:**
    *   **Structure:** First Half (Monday, Tuesday) / Second Half (Wednesday, Thursday, Friday).
    *   **News Setup Example:** News events occur on Monday, Tuesday, and Friday.
    *   **Logic:** More news events and volatility are concentrated at the beginning of the week. Therefore, less time (2 days) is required to form the weekly high/low.
    *   **Market Context:** Common during **low-volatility summer weeks** when the economic calendar is bare-bones (e.g., only 1 or 2 news events in the entire week).

---

### B. Timed True Reversals (TTR)
Timed True Reversals are intra-week reversals that occur in strict alignment with Weekly Halving Theory. Every single trading week will have a TTR because every week must form a high or low.

*   **Rule of TTR Formation:** A **single-stage crack in correlation** (SMT Divergence) is sufficient to create a TTR, provided it occurs in alignment with *Time*.
*   **Time Window Rule:** For a bullish TTR in a 3/2 split, the single-stage crack in correlation must form within the **first half of the week** (Monday–Wednesday). Once it forms, it marks a **protected high or low** of the week.
*   **Most Common TTR Signatures:**
    1.  **Weekly Cycle Sequential SMT** (by itself).
    2.  **4-hour (H4) or 6-hour (H6) Terminus Price Divergence (TPD)** (by themselves).
    3.  **Daily TPD** (occasionally, by itself).
    *   *Note:* Any additional signatures beyond these are considered added confluence.
*   **Execution Walkthrough (Bullish 3/2 Split Setup):**
    1.  **Monday:** Price prints but remains relatively quiet (no major expansion).
    2.  **Tuesday:** Price sweeps below Monday's low. At this low, a **Weekly Cycle Sequential SMT** forms between Monday's low and Tuesday's low.
    3.  **Confirmation:** Tuesday expands and closes strongly in favor of the SMT. This confirms Tuesday's low as the **protected low of the week** (TTR is locked in).
    4.  **Wednesday:** Expected to offer a protected price run to either consolidate (trade sideways) or expand higher (preferred outcome).

## 2. Video 13: Weekly Halving Theory III

### A. Projecting the Daily Candle
Once a TTR has successfully formed in the first half of the week and the protected weekly low/high is locked in, the trader's primary objective shifts to **projecting the next daily candle(s)**.

*   **Market Environment:** These subsequent daily candles are highly probable **continuation days**.
*   **The Power of Three (PO3):** The classic Accumulation-Manipulation-Distribution (Open, High, Low, Close) cycle works at its highest efficiency strictly on these **continuation days** after a confirmed TTR.
*   **Locating the Daily High/Low (Manipulation Phase):**
    *   If **bullish**, the goal is to project the exact area where the **low of the day** will form before expanding.
    *   If **bearish**, the goal is to project the exact area where the **high of the day** will form before expanding.
    *   This turning point is projected using the **PD Arrays (Premium/Discount Arrays)** formed on the *previous day*, specifically:
        *   **H6 (6-hour) and H4 (4-hour) PD Arrays**
        *   **Daily PD Arrays** (if viable, such as daily Order Blocks or daily Fair Value Gaps).

#### Walkthrough Case Study (Bullish Continuation Day):
1.  **Tuesday (TTR):** Forms the protected low of the week, expands higher, and leaves a clean **4-hour Fair Value Gap (H4 FVG)**.
2.  **Wednesday (Continuation):**
    *   Measure the previous day's H4 FVG and highlight the **0.25 (25%) level**.
    *   At Wednesday's open, expect price to draw lower (the manipulation phase) to form the low of the day near or exactly at that **0.25 H4 FVG level**.
    *   Look for a lower-timeframe SMT/crack in correlation at this level to confirm the low of the day.
    *   Enter long at the key level, expecting Wednesday to expand and close strongly bullish.

### B. Sequential PD Arrays (PDAs)
Sequential PD Arrays are arrays that form in the previous time segment/quarter/cycle and are utilized in the immediate next traded time segment/quarter/cycle.

*   **The Probability Rule:** The further out a PD Array is relative to current market price, the **lower probability** it is.
    *   *Do NOT* use PD Arrays that are 2 weeks old, or 3 days old on a micro cycle.
    *   *DO* use **fresh, sequential PD Arrays** (gaps, order blocks, or breakers that have just formed in the immediate previous cycle).
*   **PDA Cycle Hierarchy:**

| Cycle Type | Timeframe | Focus / Setup Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monthly Cycle** | ➡️ **4-Hour (4H) Chart** | Used to evaluate the week. Look for an SSMT with the previous week. High probability when confluenced with a **4H TPD** (but works without it as well). |
| **Weekly Cycle** | ➡️ **1-Hour (1H) Chart** | Used to evaluate daily behavior. Look for an SSMT between two days (e.g., Mon/Tue, Tue/Wed, Wed/Thu, Thu/Fri, or Fri/Mon). High probability when confluenced with a **1H TPD** (but works without it as well). |
| **Daily Cycle** | ➡️ **15-Minute (15M) Chart** | Used for session-level setups. Look for an SSMT between sessions/quarters. High probability when confluenced with a **15M TPD** (but works without it as well). |
| **90-Minute Cycle** | ➡️ **5-Minute (5M) Chart** | Used exclusively for the 90-minute session/quarter focus. High probability when confluenced with a **5M TPD** (but works without it as well). |

*   *Note:* The daily cycle and higher can be slightly skewed (details covered in specific live streams), but this hierarchy remains the core foundation. Over 90% of the presenter's high-precision trades are executed using this exact sequential PDA mapping.

### C. When Weekly Halving Theory Fails / Disruptions
Weekly Halving Theory gets disrupted, fails, or becomes highly difficult to decipher under three distinct market conditions:
1.  **Weeks with Bank Holidays:** Disrupted volume and timing skew the halves of the week.
2.  **Manual Intervention Periods:** Central bank actions or heavy geopolitical events override standard algorithmic cycles.
3.  **FOMC Weeks:** Because major news is released mid-week (Wednesday), volatility is back-loaded, distorting the standard distribution of standard halves. FOMC manipulation frequently invalidates WHT cycles.

*   *Trading Guideline:* WHT is not impossible to trade during these periods, but its probability drops, and cycles become highly erratic.

## 3. Video 14: Asset Synchronization

### A. Index Relationship Proper Order
While the S&P 500 (ES), Nasdaq (NQ), and Dow Jones (YM) generally move in the same direction, they maintain a strict inter-market relationship. Understanding this relationship helps differentiate between high-probability setups and low-probability decoupled markets.

*   **The Intermediate Asset Rule:** In a high-probability, synchronized market, **ES (S&P 500) must always act as the intermediate asset (stuck in the middle)**. NQ and YM must reside on the extremes (one as the strongest, one as the weakest).

#### High-Probability Market Configurations:

```
[Order 1]  NQ (Strongest)  -->  ES (Intermediate)  -->  YM (Weakest)
[Order 2]  YM (Strongest)  -->  ES (Intermediate)  -->  NQ (Weakest)
```

*   **High-Probability SMT Setup:** Proper order is respected.
    *   *Example:* If YM is the strongest and NQ is the weakest in a bullish setup: NQ sweeps a low (stop run), while YM and ES fail to sweep the low (failure swing). This is a high-probability SMT.
*   **Low-Probability SMT Setup (Decoupled):** The proper order is broken, and **ES acts as the outlier**.
    *   *Example:* ES sweeps a low, while NQ and YM both fail to sweep the low. This is a decoupled, low-probability setup because the intermediate asset has broken the correlation sequence.

### B. Non-Sequential Price Runs & SMTs
Normally, an SMT divergence signals a market reversal. However, **non-sequential SMTs fail more often than not**. We can reverse-engineer this high failure rate to trade high-probability **continuations** rather than reversals. This is known as the **"Catch-Up"** phenomenon.

*   **Non-Sequential Level Definition:** A level (high or low) originating from a time segment **before the previous one (t-2)**. For example, the session/day/week/month before the immediate previous one.
*   **Levels by Trader Type:**

| Trader Type | Non-Sequential Levels to Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Short-Term Trading** | Previous-previous Week's or Month's High / Low |
| **Day Trading** | Previous-previous Day's or Session's High / Low |

*   **The "Catch-Up" Logic:**
    1.  A non-sequential SMT forms when one asset sweeps a non-sequential high/low and the other fails to do so.
    2.  Because non-sequential SMTs have an extremely high rate of failure, we expect the market to **continue** rather than reverse.
    3.  The asset that failed to sweep the level is lagging and is highly likely to **catch up** by continuing in that direction to eventually sweep its own non-sequential high/low.

#### Walkthrough Chart Example (Bearish Continuation):
1.  **Context:** The Dow (YM) creates a stop run (sweeps a high), establishing a standard sequential SMT.
2.  **Levels:** Identify a key **non-sequential low** (from the session before the previous one) below current market price.
3.  **Divergence:** The Nasdaq (NQ), which is the strongest bearish asset, expands rapidly and sweeps its non-sequential low. The Dow (YM) lags behind and fails to sweep its non-sequential low.
4.  **Trade Setup:** Rather than expecting a bullish reversal off NQ's sweep, expect YM to **catch up**.
5.  **Execution:** Short YM, targeting its own non-sequential low as the primary draw on liquidity.

## 4. Video 15: Premium & Discount Correlation

### A. Correlated Markets & Premium/Discount Alignment
To validate any SMT or TPD setup, both correlated assets must be trading in the **same relative value zone** of the higher-timeframe (HTF) range. Failing to verify this leads to trading decoupled markets that are highly prone to failure.

*   **HTF Range Mapping:**
    *   Map the higher-timeframe swing high to swing low.
    *   Mark **Equilibrium (50%)**. The area above 50% is **Premium**, and the area below 50% is **Discount**.
*   **The HTF Alignment Rule:**
    *   **High-Probability:** Both correlated assets must be in the same HTF zone. For a bullish setup, both must be in HTF Discount. For a bearish setup, both must be in HTF Premium.
    *   **Low-Probability (Decoupled):** One asset is in HTF Premium while the other is in HTF Discount. Any SMT or TPD that forms under these conditions is decoupled and should be avoided.

---

### B. Premium & Discount SMT Local Validation
Beyond HTF range alignment, we can apply a local Premium/Discount rule directly to the SMT setup itself to determine its mathematical validity.

*   **Local Bullish SMT Validation Rule:**
    *   In a bullish SMT, one asset sweeps the low (stop run) while the second asset creates a higher low (failure swing).
    *   Measure the local swing low to swing high of the **failure swing asset** immediately before the SMT forms.
    *   **Rule:** The failure swing asset **must retrace at least 50% (equal to or greater than 50%) into its local Discount zone** before reversing.
    *   *Result:* If it fails to reach the 50% local discount zone, the SMT is low-probability and likely to fail.

*   **Local Bearish SMT Validation Rule:**
    *   In a bearish SMT, one asset sweeps the high (stop run) while the second asset creates a lower high (failure swing).
    *   Measure the local swing high to swing low of the **failure swing asset** immediately before the SMT forms.
    *   **Rule:** The failure swing asset **must retrace at least 50% (equal to or greater than 50%) into its local Premium zone** before reversing.
    *   *Result:* If it fails to reach the 50% local premium zone, the SMT is low-probability and likely to fail.
